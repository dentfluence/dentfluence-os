Daily Huddle

The ten minutes before the first patient. One screen that tells the whole team what today looks like, what yesterday left unfinished, and who owes what to whom.

In V1 Sidebar → Overview → Daily Huddle 19 screens & actions

What it is

The Daily Huddle is a single, non-scrolling board that the whole clinic looks at together in the morning. Everything on it already exists somewhere else in Dentfluence — appointments, calls, tasks, lab cases, stock. The Huddle's job is not to store anything new. Its job is to put the day in front of everyone at the same time, so nothing depends on one person remembering.

It is the only screen in the product designed to be read by a group standing up, not by one person sitting down. That single design constraint explains most of its choices: seven columns side by side rather than pages you click through; counts rather than lists; every card a link into the module that owns it.

Why it exists

The business problem, stated plainly.

Small clinics lose money in the gaps between people, not inside them. The dentist finishes a case and knows a crown needs collecting next week — but that knowledge stays in their head until someone asks. The receptionist takes a payment but no one told her the plan was ₹18,000, not ₹8,000. A lab case comes back and sits in a drawer because the person who sent it was off that day.

Every one of those is a handover failure, and every one of them has a cost you can name: a patient who doesn't come back, a payment that isn't collected today, a remake you pay for twice. The Huddle exists to make handovers visible before the day starts rather than after it goes wrong.

Above the columns

A greeting with today's date and a live clock, then a strip of five figures that link straight into the module behind them:

  • Today's Actions — total calls due and how many are high priority. Opens the Relationships board.
  • Today's Appointments — the count for today.
  • Today's Calls — reminders, follow-ups and relationship items combined, with a pending count.
  • Collections (Today) — the total your front desk should collect today, and how many of today's appointments actually have that figure filled in.
  • Pending Tasks — open tasks across the team.

Where the Collections number comes from

Worth understanding, because it is the one figure on the board that changes behaviour.

Collections (Today) is not what you have collected. It is what you have planned to collect: the sum of the "amount to collect" that someone typed against each of today's scheduled appointments. Walk-ins are excluded on purpose — nobody could have planned for them the night before.

The line underneath — "7 of 12 appointments logged" — is the honest part. It tells you how much of today's target is actually a target and how much is a blank. If that line reads 2 of 14, the ₹ figure above it means almost nothing, and the fix is a front-desk habit, not a software change.

The habit this number is designed to create

Your rule: the manager fills it in during the huddle itself, going down today's list one patient at a time. It is a judgement figure, entered by hand — not pulled from the treatment plan.

The board supports this directly. Click any patient card in Today's Patient Flow and a small edit card opens with four fields — Notes, Amount to Collect, Essential Item / Task, Chairside Assistant — then Save. One click and one number per patient, without leaving the huddle screen. Walk-ins correctly refuse an amount, because nobody planned them the night before.

Do that consistently for three weeks and the huddle stops being a briefing and becomes a target. The "x of y logged" line is your compliance measure while the habit is forming.

The one thing you can edit from the board

That edit card is worth knowing about in its own right, because it is the only place on the huddle where four different people's needs are settled in one action. The manager sets the money figure; the front desk reads the note; the assistant reads the essential item and knows which chair they are on; the doctor sees the prep item before the patient sits down. It is the handover, in one small box, done once.

Two smaller controls sit on the same cards: the status pill advances the patient through scheduled → checked in → in chair → done when clicked, and the tick circle in the Tasks column completes a task outright.

The report tabs

Your weekly read, not the team's daily one.

Across the top of the Huddle sit Weekly Report, Monthly Report, Quarterly Report and Annual Report. They are the same page with a different window — last 7, 30, 90 or 365 days — and each one compares itself to the equally long period immediately before it, so every figure carries an up or down chip.

What it triggers elsewhere

Very little — and that is deliberate. The Huddle is a reading surface. Only four actions on it write anything:

  • Ticking a task marks it complete in the Tasks module.
  • The edit card writes four fields back onto that appointment — note, amount to collect, essential item, chairside assistant.
  • Clicking the status pill advances the appointment's status.
  • + Book opens the normal appointment booking window.
  • Pushing selected communication items adds them to the follow-up queue.

Everything else on the board is a link to the module that owns the data. This is the right architecture: it means the Huddle can never become a second, disagreeing copy of the truth.

What it can't do yet

Five things, stated honestly. Items 1, 2 and 5 were settled on 28 August and have been removed from the board for V1 — they are recorded here so you know what changed and why. Items 3 and 4 stand.

1 · The Marketing column was a mock-up — removed

"Instagram – Before/After Smile Makeover · Today 11:00 AM", "Google Post – Mon Offer (Teeth Whitening) · 22 May" — these four posts are typed into the page. They are not read from your Marketing module, the dates never change, and the "+ 2 more content ideas" link goes nowhere.

This matters more than it looks. Your staff will read that column as instruction. Within a month someone will say "we posted that already" about something that was never scheduled. This is the same class of problem that got the old Communication Timeline switched off last month for showing invented patients — except this one was on the main morning screen. It has been taken off the board for V1; the slot is reserved for V1.1, reading genuinely scheduled posts from Marketing.

2 · "View All Yesterday" was broken — hidden

At the bottom of Yesterday's Flow there is a View All Yesterday link. Clicking it does not open a page — it returns a raw technical message, Not yet implemented. The screen it was meant to open exists and is fully designed; the code behind it was never connected. The link has been taken off the board for V1 and the screen stays in the codebase for V1.1.

3 · Huddle notes cannot be added

There is a notes feature underneath: a table, a save route, and a query that runs on every page load. But there is no box on the board to write a note in, nothing displays the notes that are fetched, and the save action returns Not yet implemented. As a feature it is entirely absent from the screen while quietly costing a database query every load.

4 · Comments exist in the engine, not on the board

Team comments on huddle items — add, resolve, delete — are fully built in the backend with their own table. Nothing on the board shows them. It is finished work waiting for a place to live.

5 · One made-up figure on the stats strip — removed

Under Today's Appointments the board always said "+3 vs yesterday". It was a fixed piece of text, not a calculation. It said +3 on a day you had four appointments and on a day you had forty. It has been removed for V1 along with the Marketing column.

And two things that are quietly very good

The Monday-morning problem is properly solved: "yesterday" resolves to Saturday, and to Sunday as well if the clinic actually opened, rather than showing an empty board. Very few systems get this right.

The double-entry problem is properly solved too: when a doctor records a next action at the chair, the + Task button on that patient's card disappears and is replaced by a note saying what is already booked. The front desk is never asked to re-enter what the doctor already decided.

Where the phone disagrees with the screen on the wall

Measured on 28 August by reading both codebases. Kept in this book as the specification for V1.1 — not as V1 scope.

Read this section as V1.1 input

V1 is web only. Mobile was measured again on 3 September and taken out of this release: every file in the app was last changed on 15 July, the app has no role restrictions of any kind, and the web system has moved on beneath it. The July build goes to nobody.

The four differences below are still real and still worth having written down — they are the first four items of the mobile work, dated to V1.1. One of them, the Saturday gap, matters on its own merits and is described below.

This is the first time the two have been compared properly, and the headline is better than the last measurement suggested: structurally, the mobile huddle is nearly complete. Every column on the wall exists on the phone. Tasks, labs, alerts, the patient edit card with all four fields, even the Failures/Maintenance section — all there. Mobile never had the fake Marketing column at all, so removing it from web makes the two more alike, not less.

But four things behave differently, and they are the kind you would never catch by clicking through both. They are only visible in the source, because the web board and the phone board were built as two separate programs that happen to draw the same picture.

1 · Every Monday, the phone forgets Saturday

The web board is weekend-aware. On a Monday it widens its "yesterday" window, retitles the column Weekend Flow (Sat–Sun), and shows you everyone treated across both days.

The phone does not. It asks a simpler question — "what happened one day ago?" — which on a Monday means Sunday. Every patient treated on Saturday is missing from the mobile huddle, from its follow-up list, and from its treated/missing counts.

For a dental clinic, Saturday is not a quiet day. This is a live follow-up gap, not a display quirk, and it has been happening every Monday. It is the single most important thing found in this comparison, and the fix belongs on mobile — web is the one behaving correctly.

2 · "Collections (Today)" means two different things

On the wall, that number is what you plan to collect — the manager's morning figure. On the phone, the same-looking number is what you actually collected. Same label, opposite meaning, same morning.

Here is the awkward part, and it is worth sitting with: the phone is the one that is right. It reads actual collections through the corrected, canonical money service. The web report still reads the old finance table — that is G-32 on your register, and it under-counts, because ordinary invoice payments never reach that table.

This changes G-32 from a reporting tidy-up into something sharper: your two screens currently disagree about money, today, in a live clinic. I would move it up the queue on that basis alone.

One footnote, and it does not reopen anything: projected and actual both already exist — just one on each surface. That is why the comparison you asked for felt bigger than it was. It stays dropped for V1; I mention it only so the picture is honest.

3 · The phone tells staff a note was saved when it was thrown away

The mobile huddle has a Quick Note box with a Save Note button. Pressing it stores nothing — not on the server, not on the phone — and then shows the message "Note saved locally. Full note sync coming soon."

The web equivalent is also unbuilt, but web fails honestly: nothing pretends to have worked. Mobile makes a false confirmation to a member of staff in a clinic. Whatever anyone has typed into that box has been discarded, silently, and they were told otherwise.

You already decided to hide the dead "View All Yesterday" link on web for exactly this reason. The same decision is owed here: hide the Quick Note box on mobile in V1. Minutes of work, and it removes the only outright untruth on either surface.

4 · The removals make the two surfaces closer, not further apart

Worth saying plainly, because "remove things before launch" can sound like retreat. Mobile has no Marketing column and no "+3 vs yesterday" line. Deleting both from web is not shrinking the product — it is web catching up to the honest version that was already shipping on the phone.

What this tells us about the other seventeen modules

Two of these four would have survived a screen-by-screen walkthrough. The Saturday gap looks like an empty column — indistinguishable from a genuinely quiet Sunday. The money definition looks like two correct numbers that happen to differ, which anyone would put down to timing.

So parity cannot be signed off by looking. It has to be read out of both codebases, module by module, checking three things before anything else: the date windows, the money sources, and the labels. That is the method for the remaining chapters, and it is why connecting the mobile repository mattered more than any single document.

Status

In V1, curated and corrected. In daily use at Tulip and sound — three fabrications removed on web; four parity breaks logged against mobile for V1.1. The board, the tasks, the lab and inventory panels and the report tabs are wired end to end and your team has been running mornings on them for months. What V1 does to this module is not build it; it is curate it — the Marketing column, the "+3 vs yesterday" line and the dead link are gone — correct it, so the report money row agrees with Accounts, and lock it.

Of the four mobile gaps, one is urgent on its own merits: the phone has been dropping Saturday from every Monday huddle. That is the first item of the V1.1 mobile work, and it is worth knowing today: patients treated on Saturday have not been appearing on anyone's follow-up list on a phone. The other three are a money definition, a button that lies, and two removals that close themselves.

This was the right first chapter precisely because it asked so little of you: no configuration, no data migration, one permission to grant, and a ten-minute habit that your manager already owns. It is also, now, the proof that parity has to be read out of the code rather than clicked through — which is the more valuable finding.

Appointments

The book. Everything else in Dentfluence — the huddle, the recall list, the money — is downstream of what is written here, which is why an error in this module is never contained to this module.

In V1 Sidebar → Clinical → Appointments Module permission: Appointments

What it is

A day calendar with a live queue beside it. The calendar is the booking surface; the right-hand rail is the working surface — it holds today's counts by status and the patient queue, and it is where the front desk actually spends the day.

The important thing to understand about Appointments is that it is not a diary. It is the system of record for whether a patient turned up. Every status you click writes a timestamp, and those timestamps are what the recall engine, the huddle's Yesterday column, and the missed-appointment follow-up all read. A no-show that nobody marks is a patient nobody chases.

Why it exists

The business problem, stated plainly.

A paper book tells you who is coming. It does not tell you who came, who did not, who was moved, or who left without a next date. Those four facts are where the revenue is. The module exists to capture them as a by-product of the front desk doing its normal job — moving a patient from Scheduled to Checked In to In Chair to Done — rather than as a separate reporting chore that nobody would do.

One counter behaviour to know before you train anyone on it

When a doctor viewing My Schedule changes a patient's status, the counters that come back are whole-clinic counts, not that doctor's. The numbers above their own grid briefly describe someone else's day, until the thirty-second refresh corrects them.

This is a real defect, it is not on the V1 fix list, and it is harmless as long as nobody makes a decision from a counter in the second after clicking a status pill. Say that plainly in training rather than pretending the number is always right.

What it triggers elsewhere

Appointments is one of the most connected modules in the product. Every status change writes an entry to the patient's activity timeline, and one of them starts real work:

  • Marked No Show → the rules engine creates an urgent call task for the front desk, with a three-day cooldown so the same patient cannot generate a task every day.
  • Booked, cancelled, checked in, completed, rescheduled, reverted, deleted → each writes a timeline entry against the patient. Nothing else fires from these.
  • A consultation recorded against the appointment → the appointment is closed to Done automatically. The doctor's work is what closes the booking, not a second click by reception.

What it can't do yet

1 · Chair utilisation is calculated and never shown

The system works out how much of your chair capacity today's bookings consume — booked minutes, capacity minutes, a percentage — on every single page load, and then does not display it. The only page that shows it, /appointments/today, has no link anywhere in the product; you can only reach it by typing the address.

It is also working from a default it has never been told: fourteen hours of capacity a day, and a chair count guessed from whatever chair numbers appear in the data. Before that percentage means anything, those two numbers need to be your real ones.

2 · The sidebar badge can never light up

The Appointments entry in the sidebar has a badge, and it is fixed at zero. Zero badges are hidden, so nobody will ever see it. Harmless, but do not wait for it.

3 · There is no confirmation message when a booking is made

Booking an appointment sends the patient nothing. Reminders are a separate morning sweep, not a per-booking event. This is covered properly in Chapter 8, and it is the single most common thing people assume the system does and it does not.

Status

In V1, unchanged. No W-row touches Appointments. What you see on 14 September is what is running now, with the three limitations above intact.

Patients

The register, and the profile behind it. This is the only module every other module points at, which makes duplicate and merged records the most expensive kind of mess in the system.

In V1 Sidebar → Clinical → Patients Module permission: Patients

What it is

A searchable, filterable list of everyone the clinic has ever registered, and behind each row a profile with tabs for consultations, treatment plans, visits, documents, billing and the relationship timeline.

The list itself is deliberately plain — no dashboard, no stat cards, no aggregates. That is a design decision, not an omission: the moment you put clinic-wide figures on a data-entry screen, people start reading them instead of the row in front of them.

Why it exists

The business problem, stated plainly.

Every clinic accumulates two of the same patient. Two records means half a history, a recall that goes to the wrong number, and a balance that looks settled because the money is on the other record. Patients exists to be the one place a person is created, found, and — when it has already gone wrong — merged.

Consultations & Treatment Plans

What the doctor found, and what the doctor proposed. These are two records and one conversation, which is why they share a chapter and share a tab.

In V1 Patient profile → Consultation tab · Treatment Plan tab Module permission: Patients

What it is

A consultation is the clinical encounter — complaint, findings, diagnosis, advice. A treatment plan is the priced proposal that follows it. Both live on the patient's profile; neither has a clinic-wide list screen, and that is deliberate.

There are seven kinds of consultation, five of which have their own button: New Consultation, Same Issue, Minor Visit, Emergency and COHA. Each opens a form shaped for that encounter rather than one long form that fits none of them.

Why it exists

The business problem, stated plainly.

Case acceptance is the difference between a busy clinic and a profitable one, and it is decided in the ten minutes between the finding and the price. This pair of records exists so that the finding, the option, the price and the patient's answer are all written down in the same place at the same time — so that six weeks later you can answer "what did we actually offer them, and what did they say?"

What is on the screen

CONSULTATION TAB · LEFT
The record list

Five create buttons, then every consultation as a card — type, date, doctor, and per-card View / Print / Edit / Delete. Any linked prescription shows underneath with its drug count.

Comes from: this patient's consultations only. There is no clinic-wide consultations list anywhere in the product — it was removed on purpose because nothing linked to it.
CONSULTATION TAB · RIGHT
Four support cards

PLAN TAB · FORM
Building an option

Plan name, consultation, doctor, date, expected duration and visit count; then item rows — treatment, tooth, units, fee, discount, notes — with a dental chart picker and a running total at the foot.

PLAN TAB · CARDS
The options, grouped by consultation

Each option shows its number, name, an Accepted or Presented badge, a progress chip, the estimated investment, the treatment count, and the item list. Then the four decision buttons: Mark as Accepted · Present Plan / Give Estimate · Defer · Reject.

Progress is derived, never stored. It is worked out from the clinical work actually recorded against the plan's items — which means it cannot be gamed by ticking a box.

Where the key number comes from

Estimated Investment on a plan card is the sum of its item totals, and each item total is calculated as: fee × units, less the item discount percentage, plus GST on the discounted figure.

The running total while you are typing does not include discounts or GST

The grand total that updates live at the foot of the plan form multiplies fee by units and stops there. The figure that gets saved, and the one that appears on the plan card afterwards, applies the discount and adds GST.

Consequence: if you use per-item discounts or GST, the number you quote from the screen while building the plan is not the number the patient will be shown. Build the plan, save it, then quote from the card. This is a genuine defect, it is not on the V1 fix list, and it is a one-line habit to work around.

What it triggers elsewhere

  • Recording a consultation advances the patient's last-visit date and closes the linked appointment to Done. The doctor's work closes the booking.
  • Marking a plan Presented moves the opportunity to Quoted in the relationship pipeline.
  • Marking a plan Accepted moves it to Committed, stamps the acceptance, writes a decision record, and writes the timeline entry.
  • Reverting an acceptance requires a reason, is refused outright if the plan already has invoices, and is logged against the staff member who did it.

What it can't do yet

1 · The plan lifecycle stops at "all work recorded", never "completed"

This is deliberate and correct. The system will tell you every item on a plan has been carried out; it will not declare the case finished, because only a clinician can say that. Do not wait for a "Completed" state that is not coming.

2 · There is a "suggest a plan" feature and it is not connected to anything

An automatic plan-suggestion endpoint exists in the code and no button anywhere calls it. It is worth knowing it exists only so that nobody switches it on believing it is intelligent: it is a short list of keyword matches to fixed treatment pairs, and it looks prices up by exact treatment name — anything that does not match exactly gets a price of zero. It should stay unreachable.

3 · No cross-patient view of plans

You cannot ask "show me every plan presented last month and whether it was accepted" from this module. The nearest thing is the opportunity pipeline in Chapter 8, which answers it approximately.

Treatment Visits

The clinical log. What was actually done, on which tooth, on which day — the record everything downstream counts as work.

In V1 Patient profile → Visits tab Module permission: Patients (edit to record)

What it is

A dated log of chairside work, one card per visit, each carrying the treatment, the tooth, the doctor, vitals, notes, any prescription, and the items of work recorded against a treatment plan.

The visit is the most consequential record in the clinical stack. A treatment plan is a proposal; a visit is a fact. Plan progress, the six-month recall, the implant follow-up, the lab case and the front desk's billing prompt are all created from what is written here.

Why it exists

The business problem, stated plainly.

Work that is done and not recorded is work you cannot bill, cannot follow up, and cannot prove. The visit log exists so that recording the work — which the doctor has to do anyway — is what sets the rest of the clinic in motion, rather than the doctor also having to remember to tell reception to raise an invoice, and remember to book a recall, and remember to send the lab case.

What is on the screen

TAB
Visit Log

A search box and a list of visit cards, newest first. Each card: the date block, visit type, status, treatment, tooth, doctor, complaint, notes, the vitals row, the prescription count, the next-visit date and the item count.

There is no summary bar on this tab, and there used to be. It was removed on purpose: aggregate figures do not belong on a data-entry screen.
FORM
Recording a visit

A full page rather than a modal, because a visit has too many fields for a box. Vitals, work items against plan lines, work outcome per item, lab section, implant placement, next action.

What is not on this form: money. Cost and payment are deliberately excluded — see below.

Where the key numbers come from

The vitals — BP, pulse, SpO₂, temperature, blood sugar, weight — are typed and stored as typed. The counts are lengths of lists. The one number that needs explanation is the money line, and the explanation is that it should not be there.

The money line on a visit card is a legacy artefact — ignore it

Some older visit cards show Rs. N, Paid Rs. N and Due Rs. N. Those fields cannot be filled in from the visit form at all — billing was deliberately moved out of the clinical record and given to the front desk through billing prompts. Any figure you see there came from an older import.

The line also disappears from a card as soon as anyone edits and saves that visit, and reappears on a page reload. That is a display defect, not data loss. It is not on the V1 fix list. The billing truth is in Accounts, Chapter 6 — never read money off a visit card.

What it triggers elsewhere

More than any other single action in the product. Saving one visit can set five things in motion, all inside one transaction, so either all of it happens or none of it does:

  • A billing prompt is created for the front desk for every visit with work items. This is the handover that replaces the doctor telling reception what to charge.
  • A lab case is created if the lab section was filled in.
  • A six-month recall task is created if the visit marks the treatment complete — unless a matching recall task is already pending, so it cannot pile up.
  • Implant placement and stock deduction are recorded together, once, if an implant was placed.
  • The treatment plan re-derives its own progress. The visit never writes plan status directly, which is why progress cannot drift out of line with the clinical record.

What it can't do yet

1 · The implant follow-up only fires on the exact word "Implant"

The automatic implant follow-up task is created by matching the treatment name against the single word Implant. A treatment named "Implant Placement", "Dental Implant" or "implant" in lower case is classified as ordinary work and generates no implant follow-up.

This is a naming discipline problem with a real clinical cost. If you want the implant follow-up to work, the treatment in your master list must be named exactly Implant, or the follow-up must be booked by hand every time.

2 · Three old status values can be read but not saved

Imported visits may carry started, ongoing or abandoned. The form will not accept those values, so editing such a visit forces a status change. Rare, and worth knowing when it happens.

3 · Ad-hoc work is invisible to plan progress

Work recorded without linking it to a treatment plan line, and without a work outcome, writes no clinical activity entry. It is in the visit record, but plan progress and the timeline will not see it. If a plan looks stuck at "not started" while work is clearly being done, this is why.

Status

In V1, unchanged. No W-row touches Treatment Visits. The naming rule in point 1 is a clinic decision you can act on today at no development cost.

How a payment actually lands

Two routes, and the difference matters when a patient hands over one amount against three invoices:

Finance is the canonical screen. Analytics is covered in Chapter 14 and carries its own health warning.

Revenue by Treatment Category

The one new number in V1. It answers the question every owner asks and almost none can answer: which treatments are actually carrying this clinic?

New in V1 Finance → Reports Module permission: Finance

What it is

A table with one row per treatment category and three money columns: Billed, Collected and Outstanding. It is the only report in V1 that tells you where your revenue comes from rather than how much of it there is.

Why it exists

The business problem, stated plainly.

You already know your monthly collection. What you cannot currently see is that — for example — implants are twenty per cent of your billing and sixty per cent of your outstanding, or that scaling is a third of your appointments and a twentieth of your revenue. Those are the facts that change what you buy, what you train for, what you market, and which chair hours you protect. Without this table, every one of those decisions is made on impression.

Where the number comes from

Read this before you use the report for anything.

Revenue is attributed by invoice line. Each line on an invoice carries the treatment it was raised for, and that treatment's category is what the line is counted under. This is the right basis: it follows what was actually charged, item by item, rather than guessing a category from the appointment or from the plan.

ColumnDefinitionBasis
BilledThe value of invoice lines in that category, raised within the period.Billed · invoice date
CollectedPayment received and allocated against those lines.Collected · payment date
OutstandingBilled less collected — what that category is still owed.Derived
The two things to understand about this table before you act on it

Billed and Collected are on different clocks. A crown billed in August and paid in September appears in August's Billed and September's Collected. Over a quarter the two converge; over a single week they will not, and that is correct, not an error.

Outstanding here is period outstanding, not the patient's whole balance. It answers "what has this category not been paid for out of what it billed in this period". The clinic-wide Outstanding on the Finance dashboard is a different, lifetime figure. The two are not meant to reconcile.

What this replaces — and why the old version was worse than nothing

A "revenue by treatment category" panel already existed on the Reports page. It read from a table that no part of the application ever writes to — the only things that ever put rows in it were the demo seeders. On a clean production database that panel is empty; on a database that still carries demo data it shows invented figures.

This is exactly the class of defect that the whole V1 exercise exists to remove: a screen that looks like a report and is not connected to your clinic.

How to actually use it

  1. Monthly, not weekly. A month is long enough for billing and collection to be roughly on the same clock.
  2. Read Outstanding first, not Billed. The category with the worst collection ratio is a front-desk or a consent problem, and it is fixable this week. The category with the highest billing is not a problem at all.
  3. Compare against chair time, in your head. The table does not know how long a treatment takes. A category that is 10% of revenue and 40% of your chairs is the finding, and it will not be on the screen — you have to bring it.
  4. Do not compare doctors with it. It cannot do that. Doctor-wise revenue was deliberately cut from V1 — see below.

What it can't do yet

Doctor-wise revenue is not in V1

You asked for it. It was costed and cut on 4 September, and the reason is worth recording because it will come up again: invoice lines do not currently record who performed the work. Adding that means a schema change plus a backfill of historical invoices, and it was the single most expensive item on the list. It is a V1.1 item and it is genuinely wanted — it is deferred on cost, not on merit.

Until then, the honest answer to "which doctor earns what" is that the system cannot tell you, and you should not approximate it from this table.

Status

New in V1. This is the one addition rather than a correction. If the schedule tightens, this is the row most at risk — and if it slips, the honest position is that the old panel is removed and nothing replaces it until V1.1, which is still better than a report fed by demo data.

The four compliance screens are yours. They are not part of the 14 September staff training, they are not in the Marathi guide, and no member of your team needs to open one. If a patient makes a data request, the correct front-desk action is to bring it to you — not to open Patient Rights.

What it can't do yet

Appendix B · Built, and Switched Off

What exists in the system and is deliberately not part of V1. Written down so that nobody switches one on by accident, and so that V1.1 starts from a list rather than from memory.

Not in V1 Reference

The mobile app

Not in V1. Not to be distributed. Not to be mentioned.

An Android app exists. Every file in it was last changed on 15 July 2026 and the last build is from the same day. In the seven weeks since, the web system has moved on and the interfaces the app talks to have changed. It has fifty-three screens and no role restrictions of any kind — every screen is visible to every user who logs in.

The July build goes to nobody. Mobile is V1.1, dated 30 September, and it has been redefined as a data-entry layer for seven specific jobs rather than a copy of the web system. The website and the Instagram page must not promise an app.

Case Acceptance — the patient microsite

Switched off in production on 4 September. It had been on since July.

This feature presents a treatment plan to the patient on their own phone, through a link, so they can accept or decline it themselves. The pages it serves sit outside the login — anyone holding a valid link could open them, by design, because the patient is not a user of your system.

You parked this feature on 28 August, and every document since assumed it was off. Nobody had checked. It was on, and had been since July. It was switched off and verified off on the evening of 4 September. One patient record was affected.

It is a genuinely good feature and it should come back in V1.1 — deliberately, with a decision about link expiry, rather than because a setting was left on.

Automated WhatsApp

Covered in Chapter 8. In short: no credentials exist on the production server, Meta business verification has not started, and until it completes no template message can leave the system. Three templates have a working send path waiting behind that wall; four have no send path at all. The one thing that works today is the click-to-chat button that opens WhatsApp on a staff member's own phone.

The Communication module

A full communication system exists in the codebase — threads, a timeline, an inbox. It is unregistered and unreachable, and it was switched off in July because the timeline was displaying fabricated patient conversations. It stays off. This is the precedent behind the Marketing column removal in Chapter 1: a screen that invents content is worse than a screen that is missing.

The Marketing module

Marketing exists as a module with its own permission, and the Blog Marketing Hub within it went live in August and is deliberately switched on. What is not in V1 is any connection between Marketing and the rest of the product — in particular, the Huddle's Marketing column, which showed four invented posts and has been removed. The slot on the board is reserved for V1.1, reading genuinely scheduled content.

Feature switches

The system has twenty-eight switches for capability that is built and not turned on — a rules engine, an automation engine, a workflow engine, a unified notification store, search indexing, and integrations with Google, Meta, payments and ABDM. Exactly one is on by default, the one that links a new patient into the relationship spine. Two more are on deliberately in production: the Blog Hub, and — until 4 September — Case Acceptance.

The rule that follows from this

Twenty-seven switched-off capabilities is not technical debt; it is a product that was built ahead of its clinic. But it does mean the honest answer to "can Dentfluence do X?" is often "the code exists, it has never run against your data, and switching it on is a decision with consequences".

Nothing gets switched on without a row in the launch tracker. The 4 September finding — a feature everyone believed was off having been live for six weeks — is the whole argument for that rule.
